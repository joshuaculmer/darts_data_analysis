## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (partially answerable; skill confound
  uncontrolled). Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=177.65, df=7 (highly significant at any threshold).
  By-condition means range from NONE=7.15 to BAD=9.06 pts/hit. However, this variation is
  largely explained by the execution_skill assignment: conditions with low skill (BAD=10,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=15) score highest; conditions with high skill (NONE=52, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=45)
  score lowest. The KW result confirms condition-level heterogeneity but does not distinguish
  the AI effect from the skill effect. A mixed model with `execution_skill + (1 | user_uuid)`
  is required for an interpretable answer. Confidence: Descriptive result is stable; inferential
  answer awaits model.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (confounded). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  (start bad, improve) scores 8.88 ±0.11 pts/hit vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (start good, decline) 7.22
  ±0.12 pts/hit. Cohen's d=−2.78 (95% CI [−3.17, −2.39], n=94 vs 103). Direction favors
  improving advice. **Critical update this week:** execution_skill ranges are non-overlapping
  (GOOD_PLAUSIBLE: {30, 60}; PLAUSIBLE_GOOD: {10, 20}). Since lower skill means tighter throws
  means higher scores, the full d=−2.78 may reflect skill assignment rather than trajectory.
  All 57 participants with data in either arm have sessions in both (within-subjects), making
  a skill-adjusted mixed model feasible and urgent. Until then, no trajectory claim can be made.
  Confidence: Negligible for the trajectory interpretation; the skill confound is unchecked.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟡 (skill-confounded). Finding: proxOptimal by condition:
  NONE=63.4, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=47.0, CORRECT=38.3, WRONG=44.4, RANDOM=47.0, BAD=31.7,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=12.8 px. The CORRECT condition achieves the smallest distance to optimal
  (follows AI = optimal aim). PLAUSIBLE_GOOD's proxOptimal=12.8 is the smallest of all — but
  again, this may reflect low skill (tight throws landing near whatever spot is aimed at) rather
  than AI-driven movement toward optimal. Confidence: Low until skill-controlled.

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (stable). Finding: Spearman r=+0.17
  (n=916). Higher-scoring sessions are associated with somewhat higher satisfaction. Effect is
  small but non-trivial at n=916. Within-person structure still unmodeled. Confidence:
  Low-moderate; direction is stable and sensible.

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: Trust by condition:
  BAD=1.55, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.48, NONE=2.66, RANDOM=2.64, WRONG=2.68, CORRECT=3.17,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.51. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.82). Pattern is directionally consistent:
  clearly bad or deceptive advice (BAD, WRONG) → low trust; accurate or improving advice
  (CORRECT, PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) → higher trust. BAD=1.55 is particularly low (n=47 surveys),
  suggesting participants notice and punish systematically unhelpful advice. Confidence:
  Moderate (stable across 2 weeks at this sample size; skill confound affects score but trust
  is a direct self-report less sensitive to skill level).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=−0.32 (n=739). More
  trust → throwing closer to AI suggestion. Direction confirmed and stable. Effect is moderate;
  between-condition variance likely drives most of it (low trust = BAD/WRONG conditions also
  have high proxAI). Confidence: Moderate.

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (cross-sectional). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  trust=3.51 (n=87), GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.48 (n=93). Gap is large (Δ=1.03 on 1–5 scale).
  Consistent with the hypothesis that trust at the end of a session is higher when advice
  improved during the session vs declined. Cannot assess the within-session arc. Note: This trust
  comparison shares the same design (within-subjects, all participants in both arms) but is
  less skill-confounded than scorePerHit because trust is self-reported. Confidence: Moderate
  for direction; low for trajectory mechanism.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (stable null result). Finding:
  luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.03 (n=916); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.06 (n=916). At n=916,
  these near-zero estimates are stable and unlikely to shift substantially with more data.
  Participants' luck perceptions are essentially uncalibrated to their objective performance
  in this paradigm. Confidence: Moderate-high for the null result.

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (unblocked; null result). Finding: EV grids are real
  (276 binary files, confirmed). Spearman luck ↔ evGap r=−0.08 (n=916). Perceived luck has
  essentially no correlation with actual EV outperformance. The null result is consistent with
  Q3.1: luck self-reports are decoupled from all objective performance metrics in this dataset.
  Status upgraded from 🔴 to 🟡 (real data, clear near-null finding; flagged 🟡 pending
  understanding of the evGap ↔ dispersion structural relationship). Confidence: Moderate for
  the near-zero correlation.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: The two
  mediation legs individually: (a) condition → trust: confirmed (Q2.1); (b) trust → scorePerHit:
  r=−0.01 (null). Leg b is absent at session level. A within-person mixed model might reveal a
  different picture (trust from prior sessions predicting later score), but the simple Spearman
  result does not support trust as a performance mediator at this level. Confidence: Low-moderate
  for the null in leg b; mediation proper requires the lagged multilevel model.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory (stable). Finding: Three
  clusters confirmed and stable at n=1,000: (a) trust + influence (r=+0.82, collinear survey
  items likely measuring the same construct); (b) scorePerHit + dispersion (r=−0.94) +
  proxOptimal (−0.72 with score, +0.65 with dispersion) — the throw-accuracy cluster; (c) evGap
  loading on the accuracy cluster (r=+0.75 with dispersion, −0.63 with score) in a direction
  that suggests a structural EV estimation artifact. satisfied and luck load weakly on everything
  (|r| ≤ 0.17), forming an essentially independent fourth cluster. Confidence: Moderate-high for
  the cluster structure.
