## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (confounded by skill). Finding: Kruskal–Wallis
  H=121.89, df=7, p<<0.001 (large-sample chi-sq approximation). Condition ordering by mean
  scorePerHit: BAD (9.00) > PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (8.92) > RANDOM (8.17) > WRONG (7.97) > CORRECT
  (7.69) > GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (7.23) > NONE (7.16). This ordering is almost entirely inverted from
  naive expectations and tracks the skill imbalance (BAD skill=10, NONE skill=52.5). The KW
  test is significant but tests the wrong null (all cells share the same score distribution); the
  skill confound dominates. Confidence: **The test is significant; the interpretation is not
  yet possible without a skill-adjusted model.**

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (skill-confounded despite large n).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (start bad, improve) outscores GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (start good, decline):
  8.92 vs 7.23 pts/hit, Cohen's d = −3.40 (95% CI [−3.94, −2.86]; n=64 vs 64). This is a
  large nominal effect. However, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE sessions have mean execution_skill=45 and
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD sessions have mean execution_skill=15. In this design, execution_skill appears
  to be motor noise (higher = wider dispersion = lower score): GOOD_PLAUSIBLE dispersion=37.83 px
  vs PLAUSIBLE_GOOD dispersion=13.05 px. This skill differential could fully explain the score
  gap. **A mixed model with execution_skill and user_uuid as covariates is required before any
  conclusion about the trajectory effect.** Confidence: Very low for the inferential claim;
  the confound is large enough to reverse the finding.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢. Finding: CORRECT has lowest proxOptimal (38.22 px),
  essentially equal to its proxAI (38.22 px) — when the AI is correct, following it means aiming
  optimally. PLAUSIBLE_GOOD is lowest overall (11.20 px), but this is also the lowest-skill
  (tightest-throwing) condition, so the small proxOptimal likely reflects motor precision rather
  than better AI following. NONE is farthest (62.63 px). Direction is broadly consistent:
  conditions with better advice or more compliant behavior show smaller proxOptimal.
  Confidence: Moderate (n=32–161 per cell); skill confound still present.

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=+0.23
  (n=642). Higher-scoring sessions are associated with higher satisfaction. Weak but consistent
  positive relationship; participants partially calibrate satisfaction to performance. Note that
  satisfaction means vary narrowly across conditions (3.86–4.25), suggesting ceiling effects.
  Confidence: Moderate (large n; within-person structure unmodeled).

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢. Finding: Trust means: BAD=1.56 (lowest),
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.50, WRONG=2.58, RANDOM=2.59, NONE=2.73, CORRECT=3.19, PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.52
  (highest among AI conditions). Pattern is directionally sensible: consistently bad advice
  destroys trust (BAD=1.56); improving advice builds it (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.52); the ordering
  suggests trust tracks perceived advice quality. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.81). KW-style
  test not computed explicitly for trust, but the cell means span >2 points on a 5-point scale
  with n=32–161 per cell. Confidence: Moderate; repeated-measures structure unmodeled.

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=−0.36 (n=514).
  Higher self-reported trust predicts throwing closer to the AI suggestion. The behavioral
  validation is confirmed: trust translates into following behavior. The effect is moderate in
  size and robust at n=514. Confidence: Moderate (n is large; between-condition variance likely
  contributes; within-person slope not yet estimated).

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.50
  (n=64), PLAUSIBLE_GOOD trust=3.52 (n=64). Nominally, advice improving over the session
  generates 1.02 points more trust than advice declining. Direction is as hypothesized. Cannot
  assess intra-session arc (once-per-session survey design). The trust gap here is larger than
  the score gap is defensible, since trust is a subjective measure less tied to motor skill
  noise. Confidence: Low-moderate (n=64 per arm; design confound less severe for trust than
  for score, but still present).

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢. Finding: luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.05
  (n=642); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.09 (n=642). Both correlations are near-zero. Participants'
  luck attributions are essentially uncorrelated with their actual performance or throw
  consistency. A self-serving attribution test (interaction with high/low score) would require
  subgroup analysis not run this week. Confidence: High for the null finding (n=642; near-zero
  r is robust at this n).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟢 (**newly unblocked — EV grids now active**). Finding:
  evGap ↔ luck r=−0.10 (n=642). The objective luck measure (scoring above the expected value
  at one's actual aim) is essentially uncorrelated with subjective luck perception. A slightly
  negative r suggests sessions where players exceeded EV were not rated as luckier. The evGap
  variable is no longer the flat-8 placeholder (which gave r=+1.00 with scorePerHit by
  construction); it now reflects a real, distinct signal. Confidence: High for the null finding.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡. Finding: Pairwise pieces:
  trust ↔ scorePerHit r=−0.00 (essentially zero), which breaks the X→M→Y chain — there is
  no session-level path from trust to performance to mediate. However, both trust and score
  are heavily confounded by execution_skill in opposite directions (high skill → low score;
  high skill conditions → lower trust indirectly). A skill-adjusted model might reveal a
  different picture. Confidence: n/a for mediation; the precondition (trust ↔ score
  association) does not hold at raw session level.

- **Q4.2 — Overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory. Finding: Three
  clusters visible: (a) trust+influence (r=+0.81, collinear); (b) score cluster:
  scorePerHit ↔ dispersion r=−0.96, scorePerHit ↔ proxOptimal r=−0.71 — throw accuracy
  is the dominant driver of score; (c) evGap ↔ dispersion r=+0.75, evGap ↔ proxOptimal
  r=+0.52 — the EV residual loads onto the accuracy cluster but with opposite sign, meaning
  widely-dispersed sessions score more than their aim-point EV predicts. Satisfaction loads
  weakly with score (r=+0.23). Luck is near-orthogonal to everything. ProxAI loads onto
  proxOptimal (r=+0.40) — in non-CORRECT conditions, the AI suggestion is suboptimal.
  Confidence: Moderate (n=642; exploratory, no multiple-comparison correction).
