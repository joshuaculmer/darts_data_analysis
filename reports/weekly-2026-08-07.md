## Research questions — best-effort answers

For each: **Status** · **Finding** · **Confidence**.

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound uncontrolled).
  Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=180.8, df=7 (highly significant, p≪0.001, but C3 dominates). Raw
  condition means: BAD=9.07, PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=8.88, RANDOM=8.20, WRONG=7.97, CORRECT=7.64,
  NONE=7.15, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=7.22. Ranking closely mirrors inverse skill level (BAD skill=10 →
  highest score; NONE skill=52 → lowest). The effect of AI condition per se cannot be separated
  from the execution_skill covariate in this dataset without a mixed model with skill term.
  Confidence: Low for the condition effect; the confound is the story here.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (confounded by skill). Finding:
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (PG, n=94) mean=8.88 ±0.11; GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (GP, n=102) mean=7.22 ±0.13 pts/hit.
  Cohen's d=−2.78 (95% CI [−3.18, −2.39]). The direction (PG > GP) is consistent across a large
  sample, but **skill means differ dramatically** (PG=15.1 vs GP=44.7), predicting a score gap
  of roughly 1.5 pts/hit from skill alone (based on the overall dispersion–score relationship).
  The raw effect size is not interpretable as a trajectory effect. A mixed model with skill as a
  covariate and `(1 | user_uuid)` random intercept is required to isolate the trajectory
  contribution. Confidence: Direction is consistent; magnitude is unreliable without skill control.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=12.8 px,
  CORRECT=38.1 px, BAD=30.6 px, WRONG=44.3 px, RANDOM=46.8 px, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=47.1 px,
  NONE=63.0 px. Participants aim closest to optimal under PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (improving advice)
  and CORRECT. Under NONE (no AI), they aim farthest from optimal (63 px). proxAI under
  CORRECT is identical to proxOptimal (38.1 px) — when AI is correct, following AI IS optimal.
  Confidence: Moderate (n=94–255/condition, still confounded by skill per C3).

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Spearman
  r=+0.17 (n=930). A weak positive relationship persists at large n — higher-scoring sessions
  are associated with slightly higher satisfaction. CIs no longer cross zero but the effect is
  small. Confidence: Low-moderate (real but small; within-person structure still unmodeled).

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: Trust means:
  BAD=1.53, GP=2.47, NONE=2.67, RANDOM=2.67, WRONG=2.67, CORRECT=3.17, PG=3.51. Influence
  mirrors trust (r=+0.83). Pattern: conditions with clearly wrong/deceptive advice (BAD, WRONG)
  yield low trust; accurate (CORRECT) or improving (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) advice yields higher trust.
  NONE is at the floor — participants with no advice have trust near 2.67, consistent with
  neutral/ambivalent responses. Confidence: Moderate (n=49–237/condition).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=−0.32 (n=747). More
  trust → throwing closer to AI suggestion. Expected direction confirmed at large n. The effect
  is moderate in magnitude; between-condition variance likely drives most of it. Confidence:
  Moderate.

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (cross-sectional only). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  trust=3.51 (n=88), GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.47 (n=94). The gap (3.51 vs 2.47) is large and
  directionally consistent with the hypothesis that improving advice → higher trust. However,
  this is a cross-sectional between-session average — the within-session trust arc (does trust
  rise when advice improves?) cannot be measured with once-per-session surveys. Confidence:
  Moderate for the overall direction; low for the trajectory mechanism.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: luck ↔
  scorePerHit r=+0.03 (n=930); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.06 (n=930). Near-zero correlations at
  n=930. Luck perception is essentially uncorrelated with objective performance metrics at
  session level. Luck CIs across conditions are narrow and overlapping (2.73–3.16 on 1–5 scale).
  Confidence: Moderate (these near-zero estimates are stable at this n).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (previously 🔴 blocked; now partially unblocked). Finding:
  EV grids confirmed present (276 `.bin` files, `index.json`). evGap is now a real measurement
  (not the flat 8/hit placeholder). Spearman r=−0.08 (n=930). Luck perception has essentially
  no correlation with the actual EV outperformance gap. Confidence: Moderate for the near-zero
  result; the EV grid interpretation still requires verification (see evGap ↔ dispersion paradox
  in correlation section). Status upgraded from 🔴 to 🟡 pending model-controlled analysis.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡. Finding: The two legs are
  individually measurable: (a) condition → trust (clear, Q2.1); (b) trust → performance:
  scorePerHit ↔ trust r=−0.01 (n=930) — essentially zero. The mediation leg b is absent at
  session level. A within-person mixed model might reveal a different picture, but the simple
  Spearman result does not support trust as a performance mediator. Confidence: Low-moderate
  (n is sufficient; absence of correlation is stable, but within-person structure unmodeled).

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory. Finding: Three clusters
  confirmed at large n: (a) trust+influence (r=+0.83, collinear); (b) scorePerHit+dispersion
  (r=−0.94, dominant throw-accuracy cluster) with proxOptimal loading moderately (r=−0.72 with
  score, +0.65 with dispersion); (c) satisfied and luck load weakly on anything. evGap loads on
  the throw-accuracy cluster (r=+0.75 with dispersion, −0.63 with score) but in a direction
  suggesting a structural artifact of how EV is estimated relative to aim distance from peak.
  proxAI correlates with proxOptimal (+0.41) — following a non-optimal AI suggestion tends to
  push the player away from the true optimal. Confidence: Moderate-high for the cluster structure.

## Cross-cutting concerns status

- **C1 within/between-subjects:** **Resolved.** Design is within-subjects. All 28 complete
  participants have sessions across multiple conditions. Random intercept `(1 | user_uuid)` is
  required in all models.
- **C3 execution_skill provenance:** Skill is admin-preset per session and varies within
  participants. It is a valid covariate. **Critically: skill is systematically imbalanced across
  conditions** (BAD=10, PG=15 vs NONE=52, GP=45). This is the dominant uncontrolled confound in
  all score comparisons. Every finding about scorePerHit by condition must be flagged as
  potentially skill-driven until a mixed model with skill term is run.
- **C4 multiple comparisons:** With 7 non-empty conditions and 9 variables, a Bonferroni-style
  correction would use α/63 ≈ 0.0008 for pairwise comparisons. All inferential claims should
  apply FDR or planned-contrast frameworks; no uncorrected p-values are reported this week.
- **C5 power / per-cell n:** Smallest non-empty cell: BAD (n=50 sessions). CORRECT (n=255) and
  NONE (n=199) are well-powered for descriptive work. PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (n=94) and GOOD_PLAUSIBLE
  (n=102) are sufficient for the headline Q1.2 contrast. Mixed-effect models with skill covariate
  are now feasible.
- **C6 board family balance:** Within-condition Perlin/Gaussian splits are roughly balanced across
  all conditions. Minor skews (RANDOM, WRONG) are unlikely to materially bias results.
