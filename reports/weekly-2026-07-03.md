## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟢. Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=133.0, df=7
  (p≪0.001; chi² critical at df=7 is ~24.3 at α=0.001). AI condition is a highly significant
  predictor of scorePerHit at the session level. By-condition means: BAD=9.01, PG=8.92,
  RANDOM=8.17, WRONG=7.98, CORRECT=7.69, GP=7.22, NONE=7.17. **Caution:** the rank ordering
  is largely driven by execution_skill differences across conditions (BAD: skill=10 → tight throws
  → high score; NONE: skill=52.5 → spread throws → low score). The "correct" version is a linear
  mixed model `scorePerHit ~ ai_advice + execution_skill + (1|user_uuid)` to isolate the AI effect
  from the skill confound. Descriptively, conditions with AI suggestions that participants follow
  (CORRECT, PG) score well relative to their skill level; NONE is penalized by high dispersion
  from high skill preset. Confidence: Moderate — KW is well-powered (n=702) but the skill confound
  is large and must be controlled before the AI-attribution can be stated cleanly.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (confounded). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  (start bad, improve) substantially outscores GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (start good, decline): 8.92 vs 7.22
  pts/hit. Cohen's d=−3.52 (95% CI [−4.05, −2.99], n=70 vs n=70). Direction is consistent with
  the hypothesis that advice improving over the session benefits performance more than advice
  declining. **However:** PLAUSIBLE_GOOD sessions have mean execution_skill=15 (tight throws) vs
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE mean_skill=45 (spread throws). This assignment appears systematic, not random.
  Much of the d=−3.52 may reflect the skill/dispersion difference rather than the trajectory effect.
  Behavioral data partially supports a genuine trajectory effect: PG players follow the AI much
  more closely (proxAI=14.3 vs GP proxAI=57.5), consistent with building trust as advice improves.
  PG also has higher trust (3.51 vs 2.46). A skill-controlled contrast is required to isolate the
  trajectory effect. Confidence: Low for the causal interpretation; direction is reliable.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢. Finding: CORRECT and PLAUSIBLE_GOOD have the lowest
  proxOptimal (37.5 and 11.3 px respectively), indicating throws closest to the optimal aim.
  NONE is farthest (62.7 px). PLAUSIBLE_GOOD's extraordinarily low proxOptimal (11.3 ± 1.9 px)
  is remarkable — participants in the improving-advice trajectory end up aiming almost perfectly.
  This is both an advice effect and a skill effect (PG has low execution_skill = tight throws
  near wherever they aim, and they aim near the AI which is near optimal by the session's end).
  Confidence: Moderate — directional effect is clear; skill confound acknowledged.

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=+0.22 (n=702).
  Higher-scoring sessions are associated with modestly higher satisfaction. The relationship is real
  but weak — satisfaction appears to have other determinants beyond score (e.g., the BAD condition
  has highest satisfaction at 4.26 despite high score; WRONG has lowest at 3.83 despite similar
  score). Confidence: Moderate (n=702; weak signal).

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢. Finding: Clear condition ordering for trust:
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (3.51) > CORRECT (3.23) > NONE (2.71) ≈ RANDOM (2.58) ≈ WRONG (2.57) >
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (2.46) > BAD (1.54). Trust is highest when advice improves over the session (PG)
  or is reliably correct, and lowest when advice is consistently bad. Influence mirrors trust
  (r=+0.82). GOOD_PLAUSIBLE's low trust (2.46) despite starting with good advice and then declining
  is consistent with a disappointment/recency effect. Confidence: Moderate (n=35–176 per cell;
  first-pass means; mixed model pending).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢. Finding: Spearman r=−0.37 (n=562, excludes
  NONE). More trust → throws closer to AI suggestion (smaller proxAI). The behavioral link between
  self-reported trust and actual following is confirmed at n=562. **Note:** this correlation is
  largely between-condition (CORRECT/PG have high trust AND low proxAI; RANDOM/BAD have low trust
  AND high proxAI). Within-condition trust–proxAI slopes would require the mixed model.
  Confidence: Moderate (large n; between-condition structure acknowledged).

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: End-of-session trust:
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.51 (n=70), GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.46 (n=70). This 1.05-point gap is directionally
  consistent with advice trajectory shaping trust — improving advice (PG) earns more trust than
  declining advice (GP). Cannot assess intra-session arc (survey is once per session). Cannot
  disentangle skill confound here either (PG sessions assigned lower skill → possibly more positive
  experience overall regardless of trajectory). Confidence: Low for causal trajectory effect.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢. Finding: luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.06
  (n=702); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.09 (n=702). Both correlations near zero. Participants do not
  systematically attribute high or low scores to luck — luck ratings appear largely independent
  of objective performance and throw consistency. Luck ratings are uniformly moderate (~2.7–3.1
  across all conditions) with little variation, suggesting participants may be using a narrow
  range of the scale or perceiving luck as equally relevant regardless of condition. Note: the
  luck scale may suffer from a range restriction or a general "neutral" anchoring effect.
  Confidence: High for the near-zero finding (n=702 is well-powered to detect |r|>0.10).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (EV grids operational, correlation weak). **UPDATE from
  last week:** EV grids are now operational (277 .bin files in public/ev_grids/). evGap is a
  real EV gap, not a flat-8 placeholder. Finding: luck ↔ evGap r=−0.10 (n=702). Weak negative
  relationship: getting above-EV results (positive evGap) is mildly associated with rating oneself
  as LESS lucky. This is counterintuitive but could reflect response styles (GOOD_PLAUSIBLE
  participants have the highest mean evGap=0.91 AND moderate luck ratings). The inferential claim
  awaits a within-person mixed model. Confidence: Moderate for the near-zero/slightly-negative
  finding; interpretation of the negative sign needs more investigation. Status upgraded to 🟡
  from 🔴 — EV data available, but weak signal and within-person structure unmodeled.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: The pairwise
  pieces are in place (trust ↔ condition, trust ↔ proxAI, score ↔ proxAI, proxAI ↔ proxOptimal),
  but trust ↔ scorePerHit r≈0.00 marginally — the simple correlation offers no direct trust–score
  path. If mediation exists, it operates via proxAI (trust → follow AI → aim better/worse →
  score). This indirect chain is plausible for CORRECT/PG (trust AI → aim near suggestion → aim
  near optimal → high score) but the full mediation model requires the mixed-model framework
  with the skill covariate. Confidence: n/a for mediation proper.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory. Finding: Three clusters
  confirmed at n=702: (a) **Trust cluster**: trust+influence (r=+0.82), both negatively linked
  to proxAI (r≈−0.35); (b) **Performance-accuracy cluster**: scorePerHit+proxOptimal+dispersion
  (scorePerHit↔proxOptimal r=−0.71, scorePerHit↔dispersion r=−0.96) — dominated by the execution_
  skill confound; (c) **evGap cluster**: evGap ↔ dispersion r=+0.75, evGap ↔ proxOptimal r=+0.53
  — wide-throwing participants exceed their (low) expected value more often. **luck** is isolated:
  near-zero correlations with all other variables (|r|≤0.14), confirming luck attribution is not
  meaningfully linked to any objective measure. Satisfaction shows weak positive links to score
  (r=+0.22) and luck (r=+0.14) but is otherwise independent. The trust cluster and performance
  cluster are essentially orthogonal (trust ↔ scorePerHit r≈0.00). Confidence: Moderate
  (exploratory; multiple-comparison risk with 36 cells; within-person structure unmodeled).
