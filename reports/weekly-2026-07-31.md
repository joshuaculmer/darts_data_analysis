## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (confounded). Finding: Kruskal–Wallis
  H=180.79, df=7, p<<0.001. Raw ordering: BAD (9.07) > PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (8.88) > RANDOM (8.20) >
  WRONG (7.97) > CORRECT (7.64) > GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (7.22) > NONE (7.15). Conditions with
  **lower** execution_skill score higher — BAD (skill=10) and PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (skill=15) are at
  the top; NONE (skill=52) and GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (skill=45) at the bottom. The skill confound
  (tight throws score higher, r=−0.94 with dispersion) likely explains the majority of this
  pattern. A mixed model with execution_skill covariate and (1|user_uuid) random intercept is
  required before interpreting these differences as AI-driven. Confidence: **Low for causal
  inference; very high that a strong association exists.**

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (confounded). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  (n=94, mean=8.88±0.11) nominally outperforms GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (n=102, mean=7.22±0.13) by
  1.66 pts/hit. Cohen's d=−2.78 (95% CI [−3.18, −2.39]). **The execution_skill means differ
  dramatically: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=15.1 vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=44.7 (3× difference).** This skill gap
  alone predicts lower dispersion → higher score for PLAUSIBLE_GOOD sessions. Until a mixed
  model controls for execution_skill and user_uuid, the d=−2.78 effect cannot be attributed to
  advice trajectory. Direction is consistent with the June 2026 pilot but the effect is probably
  substantially inflated. Confidence: **Very low for the causal/trajectory claim; the directional
  result will likely survive skill adjustment but the magnitude will shrink.**

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟡 (skill-confounded). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD aims
  closest to optimal (12.8 px); NONE farthest (63.0 px). CORRECT (38.1) and BAD (30.6)
  intermediate. proxOptimal measures aim choice distance from optimal and is independent of
  throw precision, making it less confounded by skill. The high proxOptimal under NONE (no
  guidance) vs. PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (improving/accurate advice) is directionally expected. GOOD_
  PLAUSIBLE (47.1 px) aims poorly relative to its final advice quality, consistent with the
  declining advice having shifted aim away from optimal. Confidence: **Moderate for directional
  result; proxAI ↔ proxOptimal r=+0.41 suggests AI suggestion and optimal aim often coincide
  for better advice conditions.**

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Spearman
  r=+0.17 (n=930). Positive but weak — higher-scoring sessions are modestly associated with
  higher satisfaction. The direction is plausible; however, r=+0.17 is surprisingly weak given
  the strong score ↔ dispersion structure. Satisfaction may track something other than raw score
  (e.g., experience quality). Within-person correlation is the right test and likely stronger.
  Confidence: **Low (weak effect, not within-person isolated).**

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptively clear). Finding: Trust
  ordering — PLAUSIBLE_GOOD 3.51 > CORRECT 3.17 > NONE/RANDOM/WRONG 2.67 > GOOD_PLAUSIBLE 2.47 >
  BAD 1.53. Pattern: improving advice trajectory (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) induces highest trust, even
  above consistently correct advice (CORRECT=3.17). Declining advice (GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.47) and
  misleading advice (BAD=1.53) produce lowest trust. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.83): same
  ordering. The NONE condition lands in the middle (2.67), suggesting participants neither
  blindly trust nor distrust an absent AI. Confidence: **Moderate — pattern is directionally
  coherent and cell n is reasonable (49–255 sessions/condition).**

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Spearman r=−0.32
  (n=801, excl. NONE). More trust → throw closer to AI suggestion. Expected direction confirmed.
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD has highest trust (3.51) and closest proxAI (15.6 px); BAD has lowest trust
  (1.53) and far proxAI (190.4 px). Note: the between-condition variance in proxAI is enormous
  (15–226 px), likely dominating the marginal r=−0.32. Within-condition analysis would give a
  cleaner trust→behavior signal. Confidence: **Moderate for direction; magnitude uncertain.**

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (partial). Finding: GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.47
  (n=94), PLAUSIBLE_GOOD trust=3.51 (n=88). End-of-session trust is 1.04 pts higher for
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (improving advice). This mirrors the score direction. Cannot assess intra-session
  arc (one survey per session). Trust recovery arc proxy: proxAI=15.6 for PLAUSIBLE_GOOD vs 56.6
  for GOOD_PLAUSIBLE — participants in improving-advice sessions aim much closer to the AI, which
  is consistent with end-of-session trust being higher and behavioral trust (proxAI) being higher.
  Confidence: **Moderate for end-of-session contrast; trust arc remains unresolvable without
  intra-session surveys.**

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: luck ↔
  scorePerHit r=+0.03 (n=930); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.06 (n=930). **Essentially zero
  correlation.** Luck attribution is decoupled from both objective score and throw precision at
  the marginal level. The WRONG condition has slightly lower luck ratings (2.73) vs the cluster
  at 3.0–3.2 for other conditions, possibly reflecting frustration with advice. Confidence:
  **Moderate for the null — the correlations are near zero across n=930 paired observations.**

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (now unblocked — EV grids active). Finding: Spearman
  r=−0.08 (n=930). Very weak negative correlation. Participants' perceived luck is essentially
  uncorrelated with the objective EV gap. This is a meaningful null: even with real per-board ×
  skill EV grids, participants do not report feeling luckier when they score above their aimed
  position's expected value. Note: the evGap ↔ dispersion r=+0.75 warrants further investigation
  (see correlation section). Status upgraded from 🔴 to 🟡 because EV grids are now real, but
  coverage needs verification (not all board×skill pairs may be covered). Confidence: **Moderate
  for the null given n=930; coverage of board×skill pairs not yet audited.**

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: trust ↔
  scorePerHit r=−0.01 (n=930) — no marginal relationship. The pairwise heatmap shows no direct
  link between trust and score at the aggregate level. This does not rule out mediation (a mediator
  need not correlate marginally with the outcome in all designs), but the near-zero trust ↔ score
  correlation makes a strong mediated effect unlikely without substantial suppression. Full
  multilevel mediation remains untested. Confidence: **Low for mediation claim; moderate for
  marginal null result.**

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Three clusters:
  (a) **Survey trust cluster** — trust ↔ influence (r=+0.83); both moderately negatively
  correlated with proxAI (r≈−0.30); weakly connected to score (r≈−0.01 to −0.06). (b)
  **Throw-accuracy/score cluster** — scorePerHit ↔ dispersion (r=−0.94), scorePerHit ↔
  proxOptimal (r=−0.72), dispersion ↔ proxOptimal (r=+0.65). This cluster tracks physical
  performance. (c) **evGap cluster** — evGap ↔ dispersion (r=+0.75), evGap ↔ proxOptimal
  (r=+0.49), evGap ↔ scorePerHit (r=−0.63). The evGap cluster is partially disjoint from the
  score cluster: high evGap (score above EV) is associated with worse aim and looser throws, not
  better performance. Lucky/unlucky items are isolated — luck sits near zero correlation with
  everything (max |r|≈0.11). Confidence: **Moderate for cluster structure; within-person
  decomposition needed before treating clusters as latent constructs.**
