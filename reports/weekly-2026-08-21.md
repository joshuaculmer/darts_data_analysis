## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound uncontrolled).
  Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=177.65, df=7 — strong evidence that conditions differ (p<0.001 by
  χ² approximation). By-condition means: BAD=9.06, PG=8.88, RANDOM=8.20, WRONG=7.96,
  CORRECT=7.64, NONE=7.15, GP=7.22. However, NONE has mean skill=51.7 and BAD has mean skill=10.0
  — the ordering is confounded by execution_skill. NONE appearing low despite high skill and BAD
  appearing highest despite lowest skill suggests the skill→score relationship is non-monotone or
  that low-skill sessions are assigned to higher-reward board configurations. Confidence: moderate
  for the overall test; low for pairwise condition comparisons without skill covariate.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟢 (well-powered descriptive, strong effect).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (improve: PLAUSIBLE→GOOD) scores 8.88±0.11 pts/hit (n=94 sessions);
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (decline: GOOD→PLAUSIBLE) scores 7.22±0.12 pts/hit (n=103 sessions).
  Planned contrast: difference=+1.66 pts/hit; Cohen's d=−2.78, 95% CI [−3.17, −2.39].
  Effect is very large, CI does not approach zero, all 28 complete participants represented in both
  arms. The skill difference between arms (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD mean skill=15.1 vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=44.6)
  is a confound — advice improving over a session may co-occur with lower-skill presets on
  higher-reward boards. The mixed-effects model controlling for skill is the essential next step.
  Confidence: Strong for descriptive direction; medium for causal attribution pending skill covariate.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢 (descriptive clear). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD has the
  smallest proxOptimal (12.77 px) — participants in the improving-advice condition end up aiming
  closest to optimal. CORRECT is second (38.34 px, matching proxAI — participants follow correct
  AI to the optimal point). NONE is farthest (63.37 px). WRONG/RANDOM/BAD are intermediate
  (31–47 px), with WRONG participants diverging from the suboptimal AI suggestion back toward
  optimal. Confidence: moderate (session-level means; skill confound partially captured by proxOptimal
  itself since optimal aim shifts with skill).

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Spearman
  r=+0.170 (n=916). Weak positive correlation — higher-scoring sessions associate with slightly
  higher satisfaction. The relationship is substantially weaker than scorePerHit ↔ dispersion
  (r=−0.944), suggesting satisfaction is influenced by factors beyond raw score. Notably,
  satisfied is nearly flat across conditions (3.80–4.28/5) while scorePerHit varies substantially.
  Confidence: low to moderate (within-person structure unmodeled; between-condition variance
  dominates the correlation estimate).

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptive clear). Finding: Trust by
  condition (1–5): PG=3.51, CORRECT=3.17, NONE=2.66, WRONG=2.68, RANDOM=2.64, GP=2.48, BAD=1.55.
  Advice that improves over the session (PG) generates as much or more trust than consistently
  correct advice. Constant correct advice generates moderate trust. Misleading/worsening advice
  (BAD) generates very low trust. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.824) but runs ~0.2 pts lower on
  average. Trust ↔ proxAI: r=−0.324 (n=804, excl. NONE) — more trust → following AI suggestion
  more closely (behavioral validation). Confidence: moderate (well-sampled, n=28 complete; mixed
  model will distinguish within-person from between-condition effects).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: Spearman r=−0.324
  (n=804). More trust → smaller proxAI (closer following of the AI suggestion). Direction
  confirmed at adequate n. The correlation is driven partly by between-condition variance (BAD
  conditions have both low trust and high proxAI). Within-person estimate requires mixed model.
  Confidence: moderate (expected direction, good n, mixed model next).

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (unchanged; once-per-session survey limit).
  Finding: End-of-session trust: GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.48/5 (n=103), PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.51/5 (n=94).
  The improving-trajectory condition generates substantially higher end-of-session trust (+1.03 pts,
  a large difference on a 1–5 scale). Cannot assess the within-session arc — survey is once per
  session. Confidence: moderate for the end-state comparison; n/a for arc.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory). Finding: luck ↔
  scorePerHit r=+0.030 (n=916); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.059 (n=916). Near-zero correlations —
  subjective luck attribution is largely decoupled from objective performance metrics. There is no
  meaningful association between how lucky a participant felt and how well they scored or how
  variable their throws were. Confidence: low (session-level; within-person structure unmodeled).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟢 **NOW UNBLOCKED** (EV grids real; flat placeholder removed).
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.083 (n=916). Weak negative — sessions with higher above-EV performance
  (more "objectively lucky" throws) are associated with slightly *lower* self-reported luck. The
  effect is near zero and could reflect that participants interpret dispersion-driven above-EV scores
  as skill (tight aim → predictable score) rather than luck. The EV-gap ↔ dispersion correlation
  (r=+0.748) clarifies the mechanism: above-EV performance comes primarily from spread throws
  landing fortuitously; participants may not attribute this to luck. Confidence: moderate for the
  near-null result; small standardized effect.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (pairwise pieces available, model
  not run). Finding: The chain condition→trust (condition clearly predicts trust) and trust↔score
  (r=−0.006, near zero at session level) is broken in the second link. A mediation model would show
  near-zero indirect effect. However, within-person trust (lagged by session) → future score is the
  proper test; session-level cross-sectional trust ↔ score conflates between-condition variance.
  Recommend a lagged within-person specification once mixed models are fit. Confidence: n/a for
  causal mediation claim.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 (exploratory, n=1,000).
  Finding: Three clusters in the heatmap:
  (a) **Trust cluster:** trust+influence (r=+0.824) — shared "AI acceptance" factor;
  (b) **Score/accuracy cluster:** scorePerHit+proxOptimal+dispersion — dominant structure, with
      dispersion most predictive (r=−0.944 with score); evGap loads here positively with dispersion;
  (c) **Survey ratings:** satisfied and luck are largely independent of the score cluster and of
      trust — these three clusters are orthogonal at session level.
  proxAI loads on proxOptimal (r=+0.412) — in non-CORRECT conditions the AI suggestion is itself
  suboptimal; following it leads toward sub-optimal aim. Confidence: exploratory; 36 cells, FDR
  correction not applied — treat as hypothesis-generating.
