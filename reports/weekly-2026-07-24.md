## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound).
  Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=185.43, df=7 (6 active conditions, df reflects 8-slot enum).
  By-condition means show BAD highest (9.07), NONE lowest (7.20). However, conditions differ
  markedly in mean execution_skill (C3), and skill drives dispersion which drives score (r=−0.97).
  The CORRECT condition (the only one with the AI suggesting the true optimum) does not have the
  highest scorePerHit (7.68) — it ranks second from lowest among active conditions. This is
  consistent with CORRECT being assigned at higher skill levels (mean=37.5) where noise spreads
  throws, reducing scores relative to BAD (skill=10, tight throws, high scores).
  Confidence: Low for inferential claims; descriptive pattern clear but confounded by skill imbalance.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟢 (robust, pre-registered contrast).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (advice improves over session: 8.93 ±0.07 pts/hit, n=94 sessions, 28
  complete participants) vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (advice declines over session: 7.24 ±0.11 pts/hit, n=102
  sessions, 28 complete participants). Difference = +1.69 pts/hit. Cohen's d = −3.47
  (95% CI [−3.92, −3.03]). Effect direction: improving-trajectory advice (PLAUSIBLE→GOOD)
  substantially outperforms declining-trajectory advice (GOOD→PLAUSIBLE) for per-hit score.
  Confounds: skill mean differs (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=15.1 vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=44.7), and lower skill
  here means tighter throws (higher score) — the skill confound runs in the same direction as the
  score gap, so the effect may be partly attributable to skill assignment rather than pure trajectory.
  The within-subjects design (paired form of the contrast) and a mixed model with skill covariate
  are the next priority to disentangle these.
  Confidence: Direction is unambiguous at this n; magnitude inflated by skill confound.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢.
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD has the lowest proxOptimal (11.07 ±1.54 px) — participants aim nearly
  at the optimal coordinate by session end. CORRECT is next (36.63 ±3.57 px). NONE is highest
  (61.75 ±4.00 px), consistent with no guidance. WRONG, RANDOM, and GOOD_PLAUSIBLE are
  intermediate (42–46 px). The behavioral signal is strong: conditions differ in how close
  participants aim to the optimal spot, consistent with AI shaping aiming strategy.
  Skill confound present (low-skill/low-noise conditions cluster near aim regardless of advice).
  Confidence: Moderate; directional finding is robust but skill confound needs modeling.

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory).
  Finding: Spearman r=+0.17 (n=942). A weak positive link — higher-scoring sessions are associated
  with slightly higher satisfaction. Notably weaker than the pilot estimate (r=+0.58, n=24), which
  was inflated by the small between-person sample. The true within-person relationship is unclear
  without decomposing the correlation. Across conditions, satisfaction is nearly flat (range 3.82–
  4.29/5), suggesting satisfaction does not track performance condition.
  Confidence: Moderate (n=942); within-person decomposition needed for Q1.4's calibration question.

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢.
  Finding: Trust means by condition: BAD=1.53 (lowest), GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.45, NONE=2.67,
  WRONG=2.65, RANDOM=2.68, CORRECT=3.17, PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.53 (highest). Influence mirrors trust
  (r=+0.82). The ordering is directionally sensible: misleading static advice (BAD) → lowest trust;
  improving advice (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) → highest trust; CORRECT has moderate trust (3.17) rather
  than the highest — possibly because participants mistrust accurate advice when they expect AI
  limitations. Kruskal–Wallis on trust would be significant (by visual inspection of CIs).
  Confidence: Moderate (n=89–241 per condition).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢.
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.32 (n=758; 197 NONE sessions + 45 PLAUSIBLE/missing excluded). More trust
  → throws closer to AI suggestion. Effect is weaker than the pilot (r=−0.45, n=19) but robust with
  n=758. The link is consistent with trust shaping compliance, though the direction may partly reflect
  condition: high-trust conditions (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) also happen to have low proxAI because their advice
  is near-optimal and participants follow it.
  Confidence: Moderate; between-condition variance contributes.

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (design constraint).
  Finding: GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.45 ±0.21 (n=95); PLAUSIBLE_GOOD trust=3.53 ±0.18 (n=89).
  Participants report higher end-of-session trust when advice improved over the session (+1.08/5).
  Cannot assess intra-session arc (once-per-session survey). The trust gap between arms is large and
  mirrors the score gap in Q1.2: participants not only perform better with improving advice, they also
  trust it more by session end.
  Confidence: Moderate for between-arm end-state comparison; intra-session arc unmeasurable.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory).
  Finding: luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.03 (n=942); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.06 (n=942). Both near zero.
  Participants' perceived luck is unrelated to their objective performance (score) or throw precision
  (dispersion). Luck ratings are flat across conditions (range 2.73–3.17/5). If anything, WRONG
  condition has the lowest perceived luck (2.73) — plausibly because bad AI advice + moderate scores
  feels unlucky. No evidence of a self-serving attribution pattern in the aggregate.
  Confidence: Moderate for null finding (n=942); within-person interaction not yet tested.

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (EV grids live; weak signal found).
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.09 (n=942). A very weak negative correlation — sessions with higher
  objective luck (scoring above EV) are very slightly associated with lower perceived luck. This is
  opposite to the expected direction. **However, the evGap metric itself has a structural issue:**
  evGap ↔ dispersion = +0.76, meaning evGap primarily captures how scattered throws beat their
  (suboptimal) EV target rather than capturing true good fortune. Until this relationship is modeled
  properly (with skill and dispersion as covariates), the luck–evGap link is uninterpretable.
  Status upgraded from 🔴 (no EV data) to 🟡 (EV live, but requires covariate modeling).
  Confidence: Low for the −0.09 estimate; structural confound in evGap must be resolved first.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (unchanged).
  Finding: The mediation path would require condition → trust (confirmed, Q2.1) and trust →
  scorePerHit (r = −0.003, essentially null). The trust–score link is too weak to support a
  mediation claim at the session level. A lagged specification (trust in session t predicts score
  in session t+1) within a multilevel model is the appropriate next step. Pairwise pieces are
  in place but mediation cannot be tested without that model.
  Confidence: Low for mediation; trust → score link near-zero at current analysis level.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory.
  Finding: Three clusters emerge from the heatmap (consistent with pilot):
  (a) **Trust cluster**: trust + influence (r=+0.82) — distinct from all performance variables;
  (b) **Score-accuracy cluster**: scorePerHit + proxOptimal + dispersion (the throw-accuracy
  triangle: score↔prox=−0.71, score↔disp=−0.97, prox↔disp=+0.67);
  (c) **evGap cluster**: evGap loads on the score-accuracy cluster (evGap↔disp=+0.76,
  evGap↔prox=+0.55) rather than on satisfaction or luck — confirming evGap is primarily
  a physical accuracy metric, not a fortune measure.
  Luck, satisfied, and proxAI are relatively orthogonal to each other and to the two main clusters.
  Notable absence: trust is orthogonal to performance (sph, disp) at the session level.
  Confidence: Moderate (n=942–1,000); treat heatmap as hypothesis-generating.

## Cross-cutting concerns status

- **C1 within/between-subjects:** **Resolved.** All 28 complete participants appear in every active
  condition. Design is within-subjects; mixed-effects models with `(1 | user_uuid)` are mandatory for
  inferential claims. Carryover effects between conditions are a live concern (not yet tested).
- **C3 execution_skill provenance:** The relationship execution_skill → dispersion → scorePerHit is
  now clear from n=1,000: higher execution_skill = more scatter = lower score. **This is strong
  evidence that `execution_skill` is an execution noise parameter, not a skill level.** It is preset
  by admins per session; it varies within participants. Confirm with the data collection team whether
  high values intentionally represent more noise. If so, the variable label is misleading and should
  be renamed `execution_noise` in future reports. It is a valid session-level covariate regardless.
- **C4 multiple comparisons:** Kruskal–Wallis H=185.43 is very strong; Q1.2 is a single pre-registered
  contrast. The 9×9 correlation heatmap (36 cells) should be treated as exploratory; no FDR correction
  applied here. Notable pairs with |r|>0.3 are the only ones meriting follow-up.
- **C5 power / per-cell n:** 28 complete participants in every active condition. BAD has 50 sessions
  total; GOOD_PLAUSIBLE 102; PLAUSIBLE_GOOD 94. For the key Q1.2 contrast, n=94–102 sessions from
  28 paired participants provides substantial power for mixed-model estimation. Smallest active cell
  (BAD, n=50) is sufficient for descriptive but not model-based comparisons until n grows.
- **C6 board family balance:** Balanced within all active conditions (Perlin ≈ Gaussian game counts).
  No board-family confound detected. Including board family as a covariate remains advisable.

## Data-quality flags

- **PLAUSIBLE (ai_advice=2): 0 sessions** across the entire dataset after 7 weeks. This condition
  arm is completely absent. If PLAUSIBLE is intended for the study, enrollment must be checked immediately.
  All analyses treat it as a structural gap.
- **No `games_played` mismatches.** All 1,000 sessions: `games_played` matches actual games array length.
- **Skill confound dominates:** NONE (mean skill=51.7) and GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (mean skill=44.7) have much
  higher execution noise than PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (skill=15.1) and BAD (skill=10.0). This is the most
  serious active confound for score comparisons. It must enter every Q1.1/Q1.2 model as a covariate
  before any causal claim is made.
- **evGap structural issue:** evGap correlates more strongly with dispersion (+0.76) and proxOptimal
  (+0.55) than with luck (−0.09). This means evGap as computed captures "how much scatter lets you
  beat a suboptimal EV target" rather than pure fortune. The metric needs covariate-adjusted
  interpretation before Q3.2 can be fully addressed.
