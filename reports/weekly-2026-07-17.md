## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound uncontrolled).
  Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=195.0, df=7 (p ≪ 0.001) — strong omnibus evidence that scorePerHit
  differs across conditions. Raw means: BAD=9.07, PG=8.93, RANDOM=8.21, WRONG=8.01, CORRECT=7.71,
  NONE=7.15, GP=7.24. **Caveat:** execution_skill is strongly imbalanced across conditions (NONE
  mean 52.4 vs BAD always 10); the observed H is partly driven by skill differences, not AI
  advice alone. A mixed-effects model with execution_skill as a covariate is needed before
  causal attribution. Confidence: Moderate for omnibus signal; low for any pairwise ranking.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (large raw effect, skill confounded).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (start bad, improve; n=98) outscores GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (start good,
  decline; n=101): 8.93 vs 7.24 pts/hit, Δ=+1.69 pts/hit, Cohen's d=−3.51
  (95% CI [−3.95, −3.07]). Execution strategy also differs: PG aims nearly optimally
  (proxOptimal=10.8 px) while GP aims 46.2 px from optimal.
  **Critical confound:** PLAUSIBLE_GOOD uses skill∈{10,20} (lower execution noise, tighter
  throws, dispersion=12.9 px) while GOOD_PLAUSIBLE uses skill∈{30,60} (higher noise,
  dispersion=37.7 px). Since dispersion r=−0.97 with scorePerHit, a large fraction of the
  observed 1.69 pts/hit gap is attributable to skill assignment rather than advice trajectory.
  The direction (improving advice → higher score, closer-to-optimal aiming) is consistent with
  the hypothesis, but the true trajectory effect size cannot be estimated without controlling
  for skill. Confidence: Direction plausible; magnitude unreliable pending skill-controlled model.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟡 (descriptive). Finding: proxOptimal varies markedly:
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=10.8 px (aims nearly at optimal), CORRECT=35.9 px, BAD=31.7 px, WRONG=41.9 px,
  RANDOM=46.6 px, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=46.2 px, NONE=62.6 px. PLAUSIBLE_GOOD stands out as aiming
  most precisely. CORRECT (accurate AI) and BAD (consistently poor AI) also aim well. NONE
  (no AI guidance) aims furthest from optimal. The confound: proxOptimal is partly driven by
  execution_skill (lower skill = tighter cluster = possibly landing closer to the optimal spot
  by chance or by following near-optimal AI). Confidence: Low (skill confound, no mixed model).

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (descriptive, replicated).
  Finding: Spearman r=+0.18 (n=1000). Higher-scoring sessions associate with higher
  satisfaction — weak but consistent across 1,000 sessions. Note: at pilot scale (n=24) this
  was r=+0.58; the true effect appears weaker than the pilot suggested. Satisfaction is also
  weakly negatively correlated with influence (r=−0.22) — higher AI influence may reduce
  satisfaction somewhat. Confidence: Moderate for direction; effect is small.

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟡 (descriptive, plausible ordering).
  Finding: Trust means by condition: BAD=1.55, GP=2.46, NONE=2.68, RANDOM=2.65, WRONG=2.66,
  CORRECT=3.17, PG=3.48. The ordering broadly matches advice quality: consistently poor or
  misleading advice (BAD, WRONG) → lower trust; accurate or improving advice (CORRECT,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) → higher trust. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.83). The PLAUSIBLE_GOOD vs
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE gap in trust (3.48 vs 2.46) is directionally consistent with Q1.2: improving
  advice trajectory drives both higher trust and higher score. KW on trust by condition would
  also be significant given the BAD vs CORRECT gap (d≈1.6/5 scale); not computed separately
  but strongly implied. Confidence: Moderate for ordering; low for causal inference.

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢 (exploratory, now robust).
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.33 (n=802). More trust → throw closer to AI suggestion. Effect is
  stable across 802 sessions vs the pilot's n=19. Correlation is modest but meaningful:
  higher trust, higher adherence. Within-person structure not yet disentangled (between-
  condition variance still dominates). Confidence: Moderate for direction and magnitude.

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (partially answered).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD trust=3.48 (n=98) vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.46 (n=101). Improving
  advice trajectory drives higher post-session trust than declining advice trajectory.
  Caveat: once-per-session survey; we observe end-of-session trust, not the within-session arc.
  Cannot assess how trust evolves during the session. Confidence: Moderate for end-of-session
  contrast; no data on within-session trust dynamics.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory, now well-powered).
  Finding: luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.04 (n=1000); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.07 (n=1000).
  Both near zero. Subjective luck is essentially independent of both objective score and throw
  dispersion. Players' luck attributions do not track their actual performance. Confidence:
  High for near-zero effect at n=1000; this is a robust null.

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟢 (now unblocked — real EV grids are in place).
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.10 (n=1000). Subjective luck has near-zero negative correlation with
  actual execution luck (outperforming one's aiming EV). Interpretation: players who scatter
  their throws widely (high dispersion) tend to have higher evGap (their scattered hits land
  in spots better than the EV of their aim) but do NOT report feeling luckier (luck ↔ evGap
  r=−0.10). Players' luck perceptions are essentially unrelated to whether they actually
  outperformed their aim's expected value. Confidence: Moderate (robust null at n=1000; but
  note evGap partly captures scatter advantage, not pure execution luck).

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (pairwise pieces present;
  mediation not yet tested). Finding: trust ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.00 (n=1000) — no raw zero-
  order association between trust and score. This makes simple mediation unlikely: a mediator
  must correlate with both predictor and outcome. However, trust does correlate with proxAI
  (r=−0.33), and proxAI weakly correlates with proxOptimal (r=+0.42) — a behavioral chain
  (condition → trust → follows AI → proxAI → proxOptimal → score) may exist but is not
  estimable from pairwise correlations alone. Formal mediation requires mixed-effects path
  model. Confidence: Low; zero-order trust-score correlation is not encouraging for mediation.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory (replicated + refined).
  Finding: Three clusters in the correlation matrix, consistent with the pilot:
  (a) **Trust cluster:** trust + influence (r=+0.83) — collinear perception items. Weakly
  inversely linked to proxAI and proxOptimal (more trust → follow AI, aim closer to optimal).
  (b) **Motor-accuracy cluster:** dispersion + proxOptimal + evGap (r=+0.66, +0.54) — all driven
  by execution_skill. Higher dispersion → further from optimal → higher evGap (scatter advantage).
  scorePerHit loads inversely on all three (r=−0.97, −0.71, −0.68).
  (c) **Satisfaction:** weakly links score (+0.18), luck (+0.12), and negatively to influence
  (−0.22); largely independent of trust and dispersion clusters.
  Luck is essentially isolated (|r|≤0.12 with all other variables). Confidence: High for
  cluster structure at n=1000; factor analysis would sharpen this.

## Cross-cutting concerns status

- **C1 within/between-subjects:** **Confirmed within-subjects.** Multiple participants appear
  across conditions (established at pilot; 31 complete participants × ≥7 conditions).
  Use `(1 | user_uuid)` random intercept in all models.

  systematically by condition: NONE uses {5,30,85,90}, BAD uses only {10}, PLAUSIBLE_GOOD

  or FDR correction. No uncorrected pairwise claims are made above.
- **C5 power / per-cell n:** Smallest populated cell: BAD (n=51). With 31 complete
  participants and ~50–250 sessions per condition, first-pass tests are powered; mixed-effects
  models can now be estimated reliably.
- **C6 board family balance:** Balanced within each condition (±5–10% Perlin/Gaussian split
  at the game level). Not a meaningful confound at current sample size.
