## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (confounded). Finding: Kruskal–Wallis
  H=88.70, df=7. Conditions differ substantially in scorePerHit (range 7.17–9.17 pts/hit).
  However, `execution_skill` is NOT balanced across conditions (range 10–52.5), and dispersion
  ≈ skill mechanically drives score. Raw condition differences cannot be attributed to AI advice
  without controlling for skill. Mixed model `scorePerHit ~ ai_advice + execution_skill +
  (1 | user_uuid)` is the correct next step. Confidence: Low for causal interpretation; high for
  the descriptive rank-order within skill-matched comparisons once modeled.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (confounded by skill). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  (n=44, mean=8.91 ±0.11) outscores GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (n=44, mean=7.24 ±0.18) by 1.68 pts/hit.
  Cohen's d=3.31 (95% CI [2.67, 3.95]). The CI is entirely above zero, showing a large
  apparent effect. **Critical caveat:** mean execution_skill for PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=15 vs
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=45. Given dispersion≈skill (r≈+0.98), the score difference is likely
  substantially attributable to tighter throws in the PLAUSIBLE_GOOD arm rather than advice
  trajectory alone. A skill-controlled contrast (mixed model) is essential before Q1.2 can
  be answered. The direction (improving advice → better score) is consistent with the hypothesis
  but currently uninterpretable in magnitude. Confidence: Low until skill-controlled.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD has the
  lowest proxOptimal (11.2 px), suggesting that participants following improving AI advice end
  up very close to optimal aim. CORRECT participants aim identically to proxAI (39.5 px),
  confirming that the CORRECT suggestion IS the optimal aim. NONE has the highest proxOptimal
  (61.9 px) — without guidance, participants aim farthest from the optimal. WRONG (40.9 px)
  and RANDOM (43.8 px) are intermediate; BAD (30.6 px) shows that ignoring bad advice leads
  to relatively good self-directed aim. Confidence: Moderate (within-subjects structure
  unmodeled, skill confound not yet addressed).

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: Spearman
  r=+0.252 (n=442). Positive correlation confirms that higher-scoring sessions are associated
  with greater satisfaction. Weaker than the pilot r=+0.58 (n=24), likely because the pilot
  was dominated by within-person variance for a single complete participant. The trust ↔ satisfied
  r=−0.157 (surprising negative) is driven by BAD condition having lowest trust yet highest
  satisfaction — participants who distrust and ignore the AI score well and feel satisfied.
  Confidence: Moderate.

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: Trust means —
  BAD=1.59 (lowest), RANDOM=2.54, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.50, WRONG=2.64, NONE=2.72, CORRECT=3.18,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.48 (highest). Direction is sensible: accurate or improving advice → higher
  trust; misleading or degrading advice → lower trust. Influence mirrors trust (r=+0.791).
  BAD's extremely low trust (1.59) is striking — participants easily identify bad advice.
  Confidence: Moderate (within-subjects structure not yet modeled for inference).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: Spearman r=−0.343
  (n=354). More trust → throwing closer to AI suggestion. Direction confirmed at n=354 (down
  from r=−0.45 in the n=19 pilot, as expected with a more diverse sample). The between-condition
  component dominates (BAD: low trust + far from AI; CORRECT/PLAUSIBLE_GOOD: high trust + close
  to AI). Within-condition slope needs mixed model to disentangle. Confidence: Moderate.

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (unchanged). Finding: End-of-session trust —
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE=2.50 (n=44) vs PLAUSIBLE_GOOD=3.48 (n=44), a difference of 0.98 points on a
  5-point scale. Advice improving over the session (PLAUSIBLE_GOOD) yields higher end-of-session
  trust. Proxy behavioral arc: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD proxAI=14.5 px (very close following) vs
  GOOD_PLAUSIBLE proxAI=59.4 px (less following) — participants track improving advice more
  closely. Cannot assess intra-session trust arc (once-per-session survey design). Confidence:
  Moderate for the directional end-state comparison; skill confound (skill 15 vs 45) may
  interact with trust formation.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (descriptive). Finding: luck ↔
  scorePerHit r=+0.108 (n=442); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.113 (n=442). Weak positive links —
  higher scores and tighter throws are mildly associated with feeling luckier. The effect is
  smaller than the pilot (r=+0.32 and r=−0.35), suggesting a weak-to-null population relationship.
  Confidence: Low (weak signal; within-person structure unmodeled).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ luck.** Status 🟡 (EV grids now present — upgraded from 🔴). Finding: EV
  grids are present in `public/ev_grids/` (276 bin files + index.json) and cover all 442 sessions.
  Spearman r(luck, evGap)=−0.099 (n=442). The correlation is essentially zero with a slight
  negative sign — participants who score more than their aim would predict do NOT reliably feel
  luckier. Residual concern: evGap reflects the gap between whole-session score and EV of the
  actual aim point; within-game throw variance (hitting better or worse than aim's EV) is still
  unmeasured. Confidence: Moderate that the luck ↔ evGap link is weak-to-null; interpretation
  pending within-game variance decomposition.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (insufficient modelling).
  Finding: trust ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.008 (n=442, essentially zero). The pairwise link is absent
  at the session level, suggesting trust does not directly predict score within conditions. The
  BAD paradox (lowest trust → highest score) argues against a positive mediated path. A lagged
  multilevel mediation model remains the correct test, but the descriptive evidence does not
  support trust as a strong positive mediator of AI → performance. Confidence: Low.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 exploratory. Finding: Three structural
  clusters confirmed at n=442: (a) **throw-accuracy cluster** — dispersion ↔ scorePerHit (r=−0.98),
  dispersion ↔ proxOptimal (r=+0.67), dispersion ↔ evGap (r=+0.76); (b) **survey affect cluster**
  — trust ↔ influence (r=+0.79), mildly: satisfied ↔ scorePerHit (r=+0.25); (c) **AI-following
  cluster** — proxAI ↔ proxOptimal (r=+0.41), proxAI ↔ trust (r=−0.34). evGap is now
  informative: it loads on the throw-accuracy cluster (not independently on luck), confirming
  that the EV gap is primarily driven by aim precision, not luck. Confidence: Moderate (exploratory).

  for descriptive comparisons; adequate for first-pass mixed models once skill is controlled. PLAUSIBLE
