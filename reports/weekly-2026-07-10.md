## Research questions — best-effort answers

### Group 1 — Performance

- **Q1.1 — AI condition → scorePerHit.** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound unresolved).
  Finding: Kruskal–Wallis H=175.49, df=7 (p≪0.001, 7 conditions excluding PLAUSIBLE). Score
  ranges from 7.15 (NONE) to 9.04 (BAD) pts/hit. Ranking by score: BAD > PG > RANDOM > WRONG >
  CORRECT > GP > NONE. However, this ranking mirrors the inverse of skill_mean (BAD=10, NONE=52.5)
  almost exactly — the "effect" of AI condition on score is largely confounded by skill. CORRECT
  (37.2 px from optimal) produces more accurate aiming than NONE (63.3 px) or non-CORRECT
  conditions, consistent with advice helping aim but not necessarily boosting score after skill
  is partialled out. Confidence: Low for inferential claims; the descriptive pattern is clear but
  the causal attribution to AI condition is blocked by C3.

- **Q1.2 — Trajectory 5 vs 6 (HEADLINE).** Status 🟡 (descriptive; skill confound critical).
  Finding: Planned contrast GP vs PG on scorePerHit: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD 8.93 ±0.07, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE
  7.23 ±0.12, difference=1.70 pts/hit, Cohen's d=−3.52 (95% CI [−3.99, −3.06], n=90 per arm).
  Direction favors PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (start bad→improve) over GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (start good→decline).
  **Skill confound:** GP was run at skill_mean=45, PG at skill_mean=15. Rough estimate from the
  NONE–BAD slope (~0.044 pts/hit per skill unit) attributes ~1.3 of the 1.70 pts/hit difference
  to skill, leaving ~0.4 pts/hit potentially attributable to trajectory — a plausible but
  unconfirmed residual. Trust (PG=3.50, GP=2.38) and following behavior (proxAI PG=13.7 px vs
  GP=56.9 px) also diverge strongly in the PLAUSIBLE_GOOD-favoring direction; these are less
  directly confounded by score-scale. A within-participant skill-adjusted mixed model is required
  for the primary hypothesis test. Confidence: Low for the score effect magnitude; higher for
  the trust/behavior direction.

- **Q1.3 — AI → proxOptimal.** Status 🟢 (descriptive, with skill caveat).
  Finding: CORRECT condition produces the closest aim to optimal (37.2 px) alongside PLAUSIBLE_GOOD
  (10.9 px — but this reflects skill=15, very precise throwing). NONE is farthest (63.3 px).
  Pattern: CORRECT → advice aligns with optimal, reducing distance. RANDOM and BAD produce poor
  aiming advice that moves participants away from optimal (proxAI=225.5 and 190.3 px respectively).
  Skill-adjusted comparison of CORRECT vs NONE would be the cleanest test; unadjusted the
  direction is plausible. Confidence: Medium (descriptive pattern consistent with theory; skill
  confound affects magnitude).

- **Q1.4 — satisfied calibrated to scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory).
  Finding: Spearman r=+0.19 (n=902). Satisfied ↔ scorePerHit correlation is positive but weak —
  higher-scoring sessions are only mildly associated with higher satisfaction. The satisfied mean
  is high and nearly flat across conditions (3.84–4.31/5), suggesting a ceiling effect or that
  satisfaction reflects something other than raw score. Notably, BAD condition has the highest
  satisfaction mean (4.31) despite being the lowest-trust condition (1.51) — likely because
  easy boards (skill=10) → high scores → satisfying outcomes regardless of trust in advice.
  Confidence: Medium (n=902; within-person calibration still needs mixed model).

### Group 2 — Trust & influence

- **Q2.1 — condition → trust/influence.** Status 🟢 (descriptive).
  Finding: Trust ranks by condition (high to low): PLAUSIBLE_GOOD (3.50) > CORRECT (3.18) >
  NONE (2.67) > RANDOM (2.60) > WRONG (2.58) > GOOD_PLAUSIBLE (2.38) > BAD (1.51). Influence
  mirrors trust closely (r=+0.82). Pattern: accurate or improving advice → highest trust;
  unhelpful or declining advice → lowest. BAD condition produces the lowest trust by a wide
  margin. PLAUSIBLE_GOOD produces the highest trust — suggesting advice that starts poor but
  improves builds stronger trust than advice that starts good and declines (GP). This result is
  not directly confounded by skill (trust is self-reported). Confidence: Medium (n=45–226 per
  cell, within-subjects; no correction for multiple comparisons yet).

- **Q2.2 — trust → proxAI (excl. NONE).** Status 🟢 (exploratory).
  Finding: Spearman r=−0.35 (n=722). More self-reported trust → smaller distance to AI
  suggestion. Expected direction confirmed at scale. The between-condition variance dominates
  (e.g., CORRECT vs RANDOM have very different proxAI and trust), but the direction is
  theoretically sensible. Confidence: Medium (large n; but between-condition variance may drive
  most of the correlation; within-participant decomposition needed to confirm behavioral link).

- **Q2.3 — trust trajectory 5 vs 6.** Status 🟡 (end-of-session contrast available).
  Finding: PLAUSIBLE_GOOD end-of-session trust=3.50 (n=90) vs GOOD_PLAUSIBLE trust=2.38 (n=90).
  Sessions where advice improved over time end with 1.12 pts/5 more trust than sessions where
  advice declined. This result is less confounded by skill (trust is subjective) and represents
  the clearest evidence that trajectory matters for trust outcomes. True intra-session arc
  remains inaccessible (once-per-session survey). Confidence: Medium for direction; magnitude
  needs within-participant skill-adjusted analysis.

### Group 3 — Luck

- **Q3.1 — luck vs dispersion/scorePerHit.** Status 🟢 (exploratory).
  Finding: luck ↔ scorePerHit r=+0.04 (n=902); luck ↔ dispersion r=−0.07 (n=902). Both
  near-zero. Luck attribution is essentially orthogonal to objective performance at the session
  level. Self-serving attribution pattern not visible in the raw data. Confidence: Medium
  (n=902; but between-condition skill variance may suppress within-person effects).

- **Q3.2 — evGap ↔ perceived luck.** Status 🟡 (EV grids now active; was 🔴).
  Finding: evGap ↔ luck r=−0.11 (n=902). Weak negative relationship: scoring slightly above
  the EV of one's actual aim is associated with feeling *less* lucky. This is counterintuitive
  but likely driven by the skill confound (high-skill/high-dispersion conditions have high
  evGap and moderate luck; precise-throw conditions have low evGap and similar luck). A
  within-participant analysis controlling for skill is needed to assess the true luck↔evGap
  relationship. Confidence: Low (skill confound unresolved; within-person decomposition needed).
  **Status upgraded from 🔴 to 🟡:** EV grids are real and cover all 902 sessions; the question
  is now answerable in principle.

### Group 4 — Mediation & structure

- **Q4.1 — trust mediates condition → performance.** Status 🟡 (unchanged; pairwise pieces
  available but mediation not yet tested).
  Finding: trust ↔ scorePerHit r=−0.01 (n=902): essentially zero correlation, which would
  imply trust does not mediate performance in the raw data. However, the skill confound
  dominates score variation between conditions, suppressing any other signal. Within a
  single condition (or with skill controlled), the trust→score path may differ. Confidence:
  Low for the mediation path; mediation model requires within-participant, skill-adjusted
  regression not yet run.

- **Q4.2 — overall cross-variable structure.** Status 🟢 (exploratory, now with real evGap).
  Finding: Three structural clusters visible in the Spearman matrix:
  (a) **Performance cluster:** scorePerHit + dispersion (r=−0.97) + proxOptimal (r=−0.71);
  throw precision is the dominant performance predictor, much more than AI advice category.
  (b) **Trust cluster:** trust + influence (r=+0.82), with moderate links to proxAI (r≈−0.33);
  trust and AI-following co-vary but are largely independent of performance outcomes.
  (c) **EV residual cluster:** evGap + dispersion (r=+0.75), reflecting how throw imprecision
  generates positive deviations from aimed-at EV; negatively correlated with scorePerHit
  (r=−0.67).
  (d) **Luck and satisfied** are weakly connected to all other clusters (max |r|≤0.19), 
  suggesting they tap into distinct subjective experiences not captured by the behavioral/
  performance measures. Confidence: Medium (n=902 gives stable pairwise estimates; skill
  confound affects between-condition variance in every cluster).

## Cross-cutting concerns status

- **C1 within/between-subjects:** **Resolved.** 45 complete participants each contributed to
  all 7 active conditions (design reconstruction: 4×NONE, 5×CORRECT, 3×RANDOM, 3×WRONG,
  1×BAD, 2×GOOD_PLAUSIBLE, 2×PLAUSIBLE_GOOD per participant). Design is within-subjects.
  Use `(1|user_uuid)` random intercept; sessions are not independent.
- **C3 execution_skill provenance:** Skill appears FIXED per condition (BAD always=10,
  PLAUSIBLE_GOOD always=15, RANDOM≈23–25, WRONG≈30, CORRECT≈37, GOOD_PLAUSIBLE≈45,
  NONE≈52.5). This means skill and condition are collinear — including skill as a covariate
  in a within-subjects model would partial out a fixed-by-design difference. The appropriate
  analysis is a condition-specific comparison that acknowledges skill as a design variable,
  not a nuisance covariate. This is a key design clarification needed before the primary
  analysis can proceed.

  pre-registered planned contrast. Post-hoc tests vs NONE have not been corrected; defer to
  Dunnett's correction once the primary mixed model is specified.
- **C5 power / per-cell n:** All active conditions have ≥45 sessions from 45 complete
  participants. The Q1.2 primary contrast has 90 sessions per arm. Adequate for descriptive
  and first-pass inferential analysis; mixed-effects models are now feasible.
- **C6 board family balance:** Board family is roughly balanced within each condition
  (WRONG is the largest outlier at 57/43P/G). No strong board-family confound detected.
